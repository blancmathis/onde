# Five relaxation worlds — Onde 1.11

Five original, continuously generated pieces: **Lagoon, Stillwater, Hearth,
Reverie and Driftwood**. Each has a separate score, instrument balance and
pattern of rests. They are not five EQ presets on the same beat.

All five are available in both **Relax** and **Meditation**. Existing default
choices, saved settings, Focus music, backgrounds and chimes are preserved.
Selecting music does not silently replace a mode's default. The star remains
an explicit choice. No new library, account, subscription or plugin is needed.

## What the evidence does and does not support

This is an evidence-informed composition project, not a clinical trial. No
paper establishes that these new pieces are optimal, superior to silence or
effective for every listener. The review below deliberately includes negative
and mixed findings. It is a targeted review of relevant systematic reviews,
controlled experiments and their references, not a claim to have screened every
music paper ever published.

### The broad and narrow evidence differ

**de Witte et al., 2020** reviewed 104 randomized trials involving 9,617 people.
Music interventions were associated with improvements in physiological and
psychological stress-related outcomes. Many trials were clinical; interventions
included music therapy, not just passive background listening. Their pooled
results do not validate a consumer app's particular soundtrack.

Source: *Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: a systematic
review and two meta-analyses*, Health Psychology Review.
https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2019.1627897

**Adiasto et al., 2022** examined the narrower question of listening after an
acute laboratory stressor in healthy adults. Fourteen experiments, N=706,
produced no statistically clear pooled benefit (g=0.15, 95% CI -0.21 to 0.52).
Heterogeneity was substantial. Genre, preferences, tempo and outcome measures
could matter, but the modest evidence base did not identify a reliable winner.
This is especially relevant to an app used after an ordinary working day.

Source: *Music listening and stress recovery in healthy individuals*.
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0270031

**Implementation:** provide genuinely different musical identities and an easy
way to choose, adjust, pause or leave them. Do not present one track as a
clinically superior default. Keep favorable subjective experience separate
from claims about blood pressure, cortisol or autonomic regulation.

### Tempo matters, but there is no universal 60-BPM prescription

**Bernardi, Porta and Sleight, 2006** measured cardiovascular, respiratory and
cerebrovascular changes while musicians and non-musicians listened to different
music. Faster tempi tended to produce more activation; pauses had particularly
relaxing physiological effects. This was a small physiological experiment, not
a trial proving that a specific tempo treats stress. Tempo was not the only
musical property that varied.

Source: *Cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, and respiratory changes induced by
different types of music ... the importance of silence*.
https://doi.org/10.1136/hrt.2005.064600

A **2025 expressive-piano experiment** with 40 middle-aged participants also
warns against simplistic mappings: slower or quieter playing did not reliably
produce lower cardiovascular activation; blood pressure rose on average during
music. The population and music differ from these ambient pieces.

Source: *High blood pressure inhibits cardiovascular responsiveness to
expressive classical music*.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-94341-2

**Implementation:** internal clocks of 48–62 BPM are artistic choices for
unhurried phrasing, not biological target frequencies. Lagoon has no audible
beat. The piano and wooden voices articulate only selected landmarks, not every
clock tick. No kick drum, driving bass rack or concert percussion is scheduled
by these scores. Rests occur between gestures while a quiet harmonic setting
continues; users can still pause for actual silence.

### Feeling better is not identical to faster recovery of every biomarker

**Radstaak et al., 2014**, randomized 123 participants to recovery with
self-selected relaxing music, happy music, an audiobook or silence after a
stress task. Music improved mood but delayed systolic blood-pressure recovery.
No single physiological variable can substitute for the whole experience.

Source: *Music and psychophysiological recovery from stress*.
https://doi.org/10.1097/PSY.0000000000000094

**Thoma et al., 2013**, randomized 60 healthy women to relaxing choral music,
rippling water or rest before a stressor. Outcomes differed: cortisol was
highest in the music group, while alpha-amylase recovery was faster than in the
rest group; heart rate and psychological outcomes did not clearly differ.
It tested listening before stress, not this app after stress, and should not be
summarized as proof that a choir reduces cortisol.

Source: *The Effect of Music on the Human Stress Response*.
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070156

**Implementation:** the choir is an option for people who enjoy that atmosphere,
not a therapeutic ingredient. Pleasantness, tension, fatigue and unwanted
attention should be assessed separately. No heart-rate or EEG scores are
invented by the music engine.

### Slow musical envelopes are not a breathing treatment

**Leslie et al., 2019** engineered ambient music with changing loudness. In a
small experiment (19 participants), modulation set at 75% of a participant's
baseline respiratory rate slowed breathing more than the fixed-tempo condition.
The silent baseline was always first, and the experiment did not establish a
universal rate for everyone. Real-time following was not automatically best.

Source: *Engineering Music to Slow Breathing and Invite Relaxed Physiology*.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08844

**Implementation:** smooth, shallow movement in harmonic color is used as a
musical texture. These pieces do not monitor breathing, impose six breaths per
minute or require breath holds. There is no fast amplitude-modulation layer.
A future opt-in respiratory interface would need its own validation.

### Binaural beats and special-frequency claims are not prerequisites

A **2023 systematic review** of binaural-beat EEG entrainment included fourteen
studies: five supportive, eight contradictory and one mixed. This specifically
concerns entrainment evidence; it does not settle every possible subjective
effect. It does not justify making a binaural layer mandatory.

Source: *Binaural beats to entrain the brain?*
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0286023

**Implementation:** no claims about 432 Hz, 528 Hz, theta, delta, "brain syncing"
or mandatory headphones. Pitch is chosen for harmony and timbre. Small unison
variations create instrumental width; they are not advertised as a designed
binaural-beat intervention. The arrangement should remain usable in mono.

### Noise and natural sound remain optional

**Alvarsson, Wiens and Nilsson, 2010** found faster sympathetic recovery with a
natural sound environment than with selected noise conditions. This does not
show that every rain sound is better than music, or that adding a noise layer
always helps. The particular sound, context and listener matter.

Source: *Stress Recovery during Exposure to Nature Sound and Environmental Noise*.
https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph7031036

**Implementation:** no forced vinyl hiss, water recording, birdsong, white noise
or intermittent sound effects inside these compositions. Existing background
controls remain independent. The app's rain and ocean layers are synthesized,
not mislabeled field recordings.

## Additional 2026 evidence

**Tan et al., 2026**, *Relaxation music for stress alleviation: Exploring
relationships of audio features and listener profiles* (Musicae Scientiae),
collected self-selected playlists from 93 participants. The clusters included
energetic/danceable, neutral/acoustic and mellow/instrumental choices. This was
observational preference research, not a randomized comparison of efficacy.
No significant overall tempo difference from the comparison dataset was found.
These five pieces explore gentle listening, not every possible way to relax.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1177/10298649261421169

**You et al., 2026**, *The structural dynamics of music drive acute stress
recovery through functional reorganization of stress-regulation networks*
(PNAS, 8 September), reports improvements in mood and autonomic recovery with
music compared to an active natural-sound control. Musical tension was related
to changing neural connectivity. Only the publicly accessible abstract and
significance statement were checked here, not the full experimental protocol.
Dynamic causal modeling is model-based analysis, not direct manipulation of
each inferred pathway. This result does not validate these particular scores.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2611584123

**Design interpretation:** develop small harmonic questions and resolutions
rather than sustaining one unchanging tone forever. This is not a reason to
introduce dramatic dissonance, abrupt swells or automatic loudness increases.
The absence of lyrics is a deliberate listening choice, not a claim that lyrics
can never aid relaxation. Smooth levels are a comfort requirement, not proof
of a measured reduction in cortisol or blood pressure.

## Musical requirements used for this collection

**Continuity without a short whole-track loop.** A sentence spans eight bars,
roughly 31–40 seconds. Harmonic choices occur on 16-bar boundaries. Chapters
span 96 bars, about 6–8 minutes. Chapter-dependent arrangement changes are small
and smoothed over 16 seconds. The tempo and stylistic identity stay fixed.
These durations are production choices, not measured clinical optima.

**Common-tone voice leading.** Shared notes keep their phase while changed inner
voices enter and leave gradually. There is no abrupt chord-block retrigger or
random chromatic leap. The bass pedal is restrained, while acoustic low strings
and piano can articulate the harmony.

**Soft, differentiated timbres.** A new sustained harmonic bed complements the
existing sampler and original vocal synthesizer. Long acoustic parts use
6–7-second envelope overlaps. Piano keeps its natural attack at a controlled
level. Wooden notes use a rounded 60-millisecond onset and longer decays than
the Focus percussion. No broadband synthetic click layer is added.

**Information, not merely volume.** The ensemble expresses a shared harmony.
Independent parts are limited; recurring gestures develop an answering phrase.
There are no dramatic swells, drops, cymbal crashes or surprise voice entries.
Lower density reduces details while preserving the sustained setting.

**User control.** Master volume never rises automatically. The existing entrance
and music crossfade settings still apply. Choosing a track after pause resets
that musical timeline without reviving a cached track. Background, clock and
chime settings remain separate.

## The five scores

| Piece | Material | Authored structure | Main controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lagoon | Original sustained additive synthesis and distant resonator blooms | Continuous common tones; only three quiet landmarks in an eight-bar sentence | Bass, Notes, Brightness, Space |
| Stillwater | Recorded soft piano with restrained cello and a quiet bed | Spaced right-hand statements and answers; low anchors only twice per sentence | Acoustic piano, Notes, Warmth, Space |
| Hearth | Recorded cellos, violas, violins, horns, bassoon and clarinet | Staggered sustained sections; no ostinato or percussion; quiet woodwind responses | Strings, Horns, Woodwinds, Orchestra |
| Reverie | Original synthesized vowel choir, recorded cello and harp | Common singers continue; only changed chord voices fade; sparse harp | Wordless voices, Harp, Strings, Space |
| Driftwood | Original wooden resonators, recorded harp and cello | Two interlocking but unhurried gestures, a sentence-level answer and decay | Notes, Harp, Warmth, Space |

Reverie uses synthesized vowels, **not recordings of singers**. Driftwood's
wooden voice is synthesized, not a recorded handpan or kalimba. Other acoustic
parts use the already bundled, checksum-verified VSCO 2 CE recordings (CC0).
No new proprietary sample bank or model weights are required.

## Continuous generation and transitions

The source has no end-of-song restart. A bounded planner creates future
sentences from authored material, seed and chapter. Themes can recur; "infinite"
means continuous scheduling, not a mathematical promise of no repeated phrase.
The new sustained voice pool is capped at 20. Acoustic voices remain capped at
64. Allocation, loading and disposal stay outside the audio callback.

The existing two-scene crossfader handles all old and new pieces. It prepares
the next scene away from the render callback and performs a bounded musical
handover, not DJ beatmatching. Meditation uses the exact Relax arrangements;
its stopwatch and configured chimes remain independent of the musical clock.

## CLI

```sh
~/.local/bin/onde music list relax
~/.local/bin/onde music play lagoon --mode relax --launch
~/.local/bin/onde music play stillwater --mode relax
~/.local/bin/onde music play hearth --mode meditation
~/.local/bin/onde music play reverie --mode meditation
~/.local/bin/onde music play driftwood --mode relax
~/.local/bin/onde music default relax lagoon
~/.local/bin/onde music default meditation reverie
~/.local/bin/onde generate set vocals 0
~/.local/bin/onde background off
~/.local/bin/onde settings startFadeSeconds 8
~/.local/bin/onde generate transition 10
```

Default commands are explicit preferences; simply listening does not run them.
Existing defaults are not migrated to the new collection automatically.

```sh
~/.local/bin/onde generate render lagoon "$HOME/Desktop/Lagoon.wav" --minutes 60
```

Offline WAV export uses the same DSP core with a fresh seed/configuration state.
The twelve-minute audition files are examples, not loops used by the app.
The exporter has a three-hour per-file bound; live playback has no fixed duration.

## Verification and future listening study

The implementation adds pure-planner tests, deterministic/block-size tests,
real sampled-instrument rendering, vocal on/off checks and live app tests.
Audio auditing checks finite samples, peaks, DC, silence, level variation and
long-range phrase development. The previous seventeen Focus configurations
were also compared against the pre-change DSP using identical fixed inputs
(16 seconds at 8 kHz, without the acoustic bank). This is a low-level numerical
regression check, not an assertion of a full listening comparison.

These checks cannot prove relaxation. The next appropriate evaluation would
randomize repeated listening sessions across the listener's preferred piece,
an unchanged control piece and silence where acceptable. Record pleasantness,
tension before/after, intrusive musical events and fatigue. Do not present an
increase in a single physiological measure as proof of benefit. A piece should
be removed or adjusted when the listener finds it irritating, even if its
spectrum or tempo looks appropriate on paper.
